Four directions
for the letterhead.

Each concept below is drawn at true A4 proportions with a sample letter, so you can judge how it reads with real text on it. All four use the corrected logo and the brand book's colours and fonts.

The current letterhead

What needs fixing.

  1. Old tagline. It still reads “his pleasures”. It should be “Prospering His pleasure on earth”.
  2. It's one flat image. The whole page is a 1,399 × 2,048 px picture behind the text, so the logo prints soft and nothing can be edited.
  3. Letter size, not A4. The page is set to US Letter, so it shifts and crops when printed on A4 paper in Kenya.
  4. The lamb sits under the writing. A full-page watermark behind the text competes with the letter and wastes toner.
  5. No contact details. There's no address, phone, email or website anywhere on the page.
  6. Off-brand colours. The green bar and the blue-grey rules aren't in the palette, and the footer font isn't a brand font.
  7. The full verse is long for a footer. Three lines of scripture at the bottom crowd the page. One key line says more.
  8. The extension line isn't used. The brand's signature graphic, the cross with its tail, is missing.

Worth keeping: the lamb and Isaiah 53:10 are meaningful choices. Concepts B and D keep them in a quieter form. If the red and green stand for Kenya's flag, concept D keeps that too.

However it looks, build it properly

A real Word template, not a picture.

  1. A4, 25 mm margins. It prints correctly on local paper.
  2. Vector logo in the header. It stays sharp at any size, and the corrected tagline is built in.
  3. Editable contact details in the header and footer, so they can be updated without a designer.
  4. Brand fonts set as styles. Garamond for headings and Calibri or Source Sans for body text, so every letter looks the same.
  5. First page only. Continuation pages get a slim header, so a two-page letter doesn't repeat the full design.
  6. A PDF version for printers and for sending letters by email.
